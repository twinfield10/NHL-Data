# Line matchups (usage plan phase C, 2026-10-07)

5v5, 2015-16 to 2025-26 (395 team-seasons). Tiers are ice-time tiers from
`processed/usage`. The interaction test uses each stint's point-in-time residual against the
additive M3 prediction (`nhl.usage.onice.stint_predictions`). Tables:
[usage-matchups-numbers.md](usage-matchups-numbers.md), regenerated by `nhl usage-matchups`.
Code: `src/nhl/usage/matchups.py`. Per-team matching tables for the site:
`processed/usage_matchups/{season}` (team, coach, venue × own tier × opponent tier).

## Verdict
**Matchups are real but don't move game prices. The simulator is unchanged** (the plan's
Phase C gate). Coaches clearly match lines, and the home team uses its last change. But
once each player's rating is known, who faces whom adds almost nothing to the xG of a stint.
Summed over a game under actual deployment, it shifts a team's expected xG by 0.0006 on
average (0.012 at the 99th percentile). Matchup data stays descriptive: player and team
pages, and the context input to player projections (Phase D).

## 1. How much matching is there?
Ratio = how often a tier faces an opponent tier relative to no matching (1.0).
- **Like plays like.** F1 vs F1 1.18, F4 vs F4 1.58; F1 vs F4 0.78. Part of this is
  mechanical: both benches roll lines in order after whistles. The forward-vs-forward table
  is symmetric, so it can't say which coach is doing it.
- **The last change shows up on defence.** At home, the top pair faces the opponent's top
  line at 1.16× (away 1.10×) and the third pair is sheltered from it, 0.75× (away 0.83×).
  For 76% of team-seasons, D1-vs-F1 is higher at home than on the road.
- **Teams differ.** Forward-matching intensity (mutual information between own and opponent
  tier) runs from 0.009 to 0.039 bits (10th to 90th percentile of team-seasons). That's the
  coach matching index for team pages.

## 2. Who gets the hard minutes?
Standardized regression of a skater's competition quality (QoC net) on his tier, ratings and
DZ-start share (skater-team-seasons with ≥ 200 5v5 minutes):
- **Tier explains most of it** (coefficient −0.76 for forwards, −0.58 for D; R² 0.52 / 0.42).
- **Beyond tier, defensive-zone starts go with tougher competition** (+0.17 F, +0.18 D):
  defensive deployments come as a package, DZ draws against top lines.
- **Defensive ability beyond tier barely matters** (+0.05 F, +0.08 D). Coaches match by line
  more than by individual defensive skill.

## 3. Is there an interaction?
Leave one season out, held-out weighted MSE of stint residuals, against tier main effects:
- **Tier × tier cells: no.** Mean gain −0.000001 (positive in 3 of 11 seasons).
- **Offence × defence product: yes, but tiny.** Gain +0.000005 ± 0.000001, positive in 10 of
  11 seasons. Good attacks gain a little more against weak defenders and lose a little more
  against strong ones (2025-26: γ = 0.22; ±0.026 xG/60 at one standard deviation of each),
  so xG is slightly multiplicative rather than purely additive.
- **Impact on games:** because the product depends on who is on the ice together, actual
  deployment vs no matching changes a team's expected 5v5 xG by −0.0006 per game on average
  (sd 0.003, 99th percentile 0.012). Too small to price.

This agrees with M4's rejected style-matchup test: hockey matchups add little beyond the
additive ratings.

## Follow-ups
- Coach matching index and the per-team matrix on team pages (Phase E).
- The product term could be revisited if M3 ever moves to a multiplicative (log-link) model,
  where it would be absorbed rather than added.
